petrichor /ˈpɛtɹɪkɔː(ɹ)/
noun
1
The distinctive scent, usually described as pleasant, that accompanies the first rain after a long warm dry spell.
“The petrichor rose from the pavement after the first summer storm.”
2
The oil exuded by plants during dry periods, absorbed by rocks and released by rain.
Origin
Coined in 1964 by Isabel Bear and Richard Thomas, from petro- (“stone”) + ichor.
